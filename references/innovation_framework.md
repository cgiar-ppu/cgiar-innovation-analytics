# CGIAR Innovation and Scaling Readiness Framework

## What is an "Innovation" in CGIAR?

In the CGIAR context, an innovation is a knowledge, technology, or practice (new or adapted) that has been developed, tested, and validated to achieve specific development impact. Innovations range from crop varieties and farming practices to policy frameworks, digital tools, and capacity development approaches.

CGIAR tracks innovations through the Performance and Results Management System (PRMS) as `innovation_development` results. As of the latest database snapshot, there are **5,236 innovation development results** across all initiatives and science programmes.

## Innovation Types

CGIAR classifies innovations into four types (from CLARISA):

| Code | Type | Definition |
|------|------|-----------|
| 12 | **Technological innovation** | Innovations of technical/material nature, including varieties/breeds; crop and livestock management practices; machines; processing technologies; big data and information systems |
| 13 | **Capacity development innovation** | Innovations that strengthen capacity, including farmer, extension or investor decision-support services; accelerator/incubator programs; manuals, training programs and curricula; online courses |
| 14 | **Policy, organizational or institutional innovation** | Innovations that create enabling conditions, including policy, legal and regulatory frameworks; business models; finance mechanisms; partnership models; public/private delivery strategies |
| 15 | **Other / Not classified** | Unknown or the type does not work for the innovation |

## Innovation Characteristics

Innovations are further classified by their degree of disruption:

| ID | Characteristic | Definition |
|----|---------------|-----------|
| 1 | **Incremental** | Innovations that already exist and undergo constant, steady progress and improvement |
| 2 | **Radical** | Innovations that are new and replace existing products, systems, services and/or policies but do not cause or require major reconfiguration of farming, market and/or policy/business models |
| 3 | **Disruptive** | Innovations that are new and cause or require major reconfiguration of farming, market and/or policy/business models |
| 4 | **Other** | Unknown or the characterization does not work for the innovation |

## Innovation Readiness Levels (IRL)

CGIAR uses a 10-point scale (0-9) for assessing how ready an innovation is for scaling. This is derived from the Technology Readiness Level (TRL) concept but broadened to include social, institutional, and market readiness dimensions.

| Level | Name | Definition | Stage |
|-------|------|-----------|-------|
| 0 | Idea | The innovation is at idea stage | Discovery |
| 1 | Basic Research | The innovation's basic principles are being researched for their ability to achieve a specific impact | Discovery |
| 2 | Formulation | The innovation's key concepts are being formulated or designed | Discovery |
| 3 | Proof of Concept | The innovation's key concepts have been validated for their ability to achieve a specific impact | Proof of Concept |
| 4 | Controlled Testing | The innovation is being tested for its ability to achieve a specific impact under fully-controlled conditions | Piloting |
| 5 | Model/Early Prototype | The innovation is validated for its ability to achieve a specific impact under fully controlled conditions | Piloting |
| 6 | Semi-Controlled Testing | The innovation is being tested for its ability to achieve a specific impact under semi-controlled conditions | Piloting |
| 7 | Prototype | The innovation is validated for its ability to achieve a specific impact under semi-controlled conditions | Scaling Ready |
| 8 | Uncontrolled Testing | The innovation is being tested for its ability to achieve a specific impact under uncontrolled conditions | Scaling |
| 9 | Proven Innovation | The innovation is validated for its ability to achieve a specific impact under uncontrolled conditions | Scaling |

**Key threshold:** Level 7 (Prototype) is the critical maturity threshold. Innovations at IRL 7+ are considered validated for scaling and eligible for investment in scaling strategies.

## Innovation Use Levels

Beyond development readiness, CGIAR also tracks how broadly innovations are being used:

| Level | User Category | Definition |
|-------|--------------|-----------|
| 0 | No use | Innovation is not used |
| 1 | Project lead organization | Innovation is used by organization(s) leading the innovation development |
| 2 | Partners (some) | Innovation is used by some partners involved in initial innovation development |
| 3 | Partners (common) | Innovation is commonly used by partners involved in initial innovation development |
| 4 | Connected next-user (some) | Innovation is used by some organizations connected to partners involved in the initial innovation development |
| 5 | Connected next-user (common) | Innovation is commonly used by organizations connected to partners involved in the initial innovation development |
| 6 | Unconnected next-user (some) | Innovation is used by organizations not connected to partners involved in the initial innovation development |
| 7 | Unconnected next-user (common) | Innovation is commonly used by organizations not connected to partners involved in the initial innovation development |
| 8 | End-user / Beneficiaries (some) | Innovation is used by some end-users or beneficiaries who were not involved in the initial innovation development |
| 9 | End-user / Beneficiaries (common) | Innovation is commonly used by end-users or beneficiaries who were not involved in the initial innovation development |

**User types tracked:** Farmers/(agro)pastoralists/herders/fishers, Researchers, Extension agents, Policy actors (public or private), Other.

## Innovation Packages and Scaling Readiness (IPSR)

An Innovation Package is a bundle of complementary innovations designed to achieve impact at scale. Rather than scaling a single technology in isolation, CGIAR recognizes that successful scaling typically requires combining core innovations with complementary ones that address barriers in the enabling environment.

PRMS tracks **255 Innovation Packages** and **612 Complementary Innovations** linked to them.

### The Scaling Readiness Framework

The Scaling Readiness framework is CGIAR's systematic approach to assessing and improving the scaling potential of innovations. It has three phases:

#### Phase 1: Identify
- Characterize the core innovation and its complementary innovations
- Map the innovation package components
- Identify the scaling context (geography, target users, enabling environment)

#### Phase 2: Assess and Optimize
- Score each innovation in the package on readiness (IRL 0-9) and use (0-9)
- Identify bottlenecks -- innovations with low readiness or low use that constrain the whole package
- Develop strategies to overcome bottlenecks (e.g., policy advocacy, training, partnerships)
- Optimize the innovation package composition

#### Phase 3: Translate
- Develop the Scaling Delivery Strategy -- a comprehensive 14-section document
- Engage scaling partners and delivery actors
- Build the business case and financial sustainability plan
- Execute the scaling pathway

### Scaling Delivery Strategy

The Scaling Delivery Strategy is the primary output of the scaling readiness process. It comprises 14 sections plus an Executive Summary:

| Section | Title | Content |
|---------|-------|---------|
| 1 | Problem Statement and Existing Demand | 5W analysis of the problem, country context, demand signals |
| 2 | Core Innovation and Scaling Readiness | Innovation history, IPSR readiness, scaling potential |
| 3 | Barriers to Scale | Barrier-by-barrier analysis from IPSR workshops |
| 4 | Scaling Ambition and Target Groups | Scaling goals, market segments, target populations |
| 5 | Responsible Scaling Principles | Gender equity, environmental sustainability, do-no-harm, co-design |
| 6 | Enabling Environment | Policy, institutions, infrastructure analysis |
| 7 | Expected Benefits for End Users | Cost-benefit analysis, economic and non-economic benefits |
| 8 | Scaling Ambition (Strategic Alignment) | Alignment with CGIAR Impact Areas, SDGs, national priorities |
| 9 | Theory of Change | Causal chain from activities to impact |
| 10 | Timeline and Pathways to Scale | 15-year phased roadmap with milestones |
| 11 | Organization of Roles | Partner roles, activities, CGIAR exit strategy |
| 12 | Cost Structure and Financial Sustainability | Cost analysis, ROI, sustainability plan |
| 13 | Environmental Sustainability | Climate risk, ecological impact, carbon footprint |
| 14 | Business Case | 10-year business strategy with revenue model |

### Scaling Pathways

Innovations can follow three scaling pathway typologies:

1. **Public-sector led** -- Government agencies drive adoption through policies, extension services, and public programs
2. **Market-led** -- Private sector actors drive adoption through commercial channels and market demand
3. **Public-Private Partnerships (PPPs)** -- Hybrid approaches combining public policy and private delivery

The pathway may change across phases of the scaling journey. The framework distinguishes between:
- **Scaling within** -- Deepening adoption in current geographies and user groups
- **Scaling beyond** -- Expanding to new geographies, markets, or user populations

## Screening for Scaling Eligibility

Before an innovation enters the full scaling strategy process, it undergoes screening against 11 criteria organized into 4 domains:

### Mandatory Gate (all 3 must be met)
1. **Clear Problem Definition** -- Named problem with quantified scope
2. **Defined Innovation** -- Named innovation with distinguishing features
3. **Evidence of Effectiveness** -- Documented evidence (peer-reviewed, pilot results, or IRL 7+)

### Threshold Gate (5 of 8 must be met for eligibility)
4. Evidence of Demand or User Need
5. Explicit Scaling Ambition (quantified targets)
6. Identified Stakeholders or Delivery Actors
7. Financing Approach for Scaling
8. Defined Geographic or System Scope
9. Potential Scale of Impact
10. Risk or Constraint Awareness
11. Social, Gender, and/or Environmental Consideration

**Decision logic:** 5+ threshold criteria met = Eligible; exactly 4 = Conditionally Eligible; 0-3 = Not Yet Eligible.

## How Innovations are Tracked in PRMS

Innovation results in PRMS include:

- **Title and description** of the innovation
- **Innovation type** (technological, capacity development, policy/institutional, other)
- **Innovation readiness level** (0-9 scale)
- **Geographic scope** (global to sub-national)
- **Contributing initiative/programme** (mapped to Theory of Change)
- **Lead center** and contributing centers
- **External partners** involved in development
- **Evidence links** supporting the readiness level claim
- **Bilateral project** funding the work
- **Cross-cutting tags** (gender, climate, nutrition, environment, poverty)

Innovation use results additionally track:
- **User type** and number of users (with gender disaggregation)
- **Quantitative measures** of use (e.g., hectares, districts)
- **Evidence** supporting reported usage numbers
